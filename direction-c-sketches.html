E1 · Direction C · Sketches

One clean event. An explosion of coordination. A closure that can't find its pieces.

A CAPA enters the system of record as a single clean entry. Between opening and closure it fragments across SharePoint drafts, Teams threads, Outlook chains, Excel trackers, and meetings with no notes. To close the loop, someone has to find every piece again. Many pieces never return.

Four structural takes on the same three-beat story. Enter. Explode. Reassemble (or fail to). Same node vocabulary as linear-flow, Direction D, and Direction B. SharePoint, Teams, Outlook, Excel, Meeting. Microsoft-stack only. E1 shows the problem only; the governed thread is E2.

5-second read

One card on the left. A fan of fragments radiating outward. A broken closure card on the right with "18 of 28 found." The fan shape says "explosion," the orphan counter says "you can't close what you can't find."

Animation hook

Entry card settles. The pulse ring at x=320 throbs once. Five vectors draw outward simultaneously, each a staggered stroke animation. Fragments materialise along vectors. Finally, closure node pulls magnetically at connected fragments; orphan count ticks up.

Risks

Most decorative of the four. The fan can look like a fireworks poster if the connectors are too prominent. Keep vectors thin-dashed, let the cards carry the read. The orphan counter is load-bearing and must be legible at scroll speed.

5-second read

A deterministic tree, left to right. One becomes five, becomes fifteen, becomes thirty. Closure on the right fails to gather eight leaves. The shape is a classic fractal and the numbers on the right make it bite.

Animation hook

Entry card settles. L1 branches draw out, five tool nodes appear. L2 branches cascade, 15 sub-nodes. L3 leaves rain in. A second later, the closure gather box activates and faint gather-lines pull toward some leaves. Orphan counter increments to eight.

Risks

Looks like an ML model diagram or an org chart if the leaves stay too regular. Keep the leaves slightly jittered and the connectors faint. The read must be "chaos that looks organised" not "organisation with variance."

5-second read

A river delta seen from above. One stream entering, twenty-four streams exiting. The closure box on the right can only reach twelve. The rest simply run off the edge.

Animation hook

Single thread draws in from the left. At each fork point, the line visibly splits and new branches extend. After the fourth fork, terminals twinkle to life. The closure box wakes up and five pull-lines reach toward nearby terminals. Counter settles at 12 / 24.

Risks

Closest risk to "chart" territory. Mitigate with irregular vertical spacing and tool-tinted dots so it reads as coordination, not data. The delta metaphor is less obvious than iceberg or tree, may need one line of ground-truth copy to land.

5-second read

A big closure card on the right, with hard numbers. Fragments on the left, most of them magnetically linked. Grey cards floating at the edges labelled ORPHAN. 14 of 42 never came back.

Animation hook

Closure card appears first, numbers both at zero. Fragments materialise across the canvas in a soft cascade. Magnetic lines pulse outward from closure, latching onto reachable fragments while the linked count ticks up. Orphans briefly flash, then fade to grey. Final state settles at 28 linked, 14 orphan.

Risks

Leans on the orphan label to carry meaning. Without it the grey cards look like "just more cards." Label must stay on. This is also the most CFO-friendly of the four sketches because the numbers are the focal point, which may or may not be what Ben wants for the hero.

Design language rules these sketches follow

  • Same node vocabulary as linear-flow, Direction D, and Direction B. Rounded-rect cards, thin borders, monospace labels. Consistency across the four explorations is intentional.
  • Tools shown: SharePoint, Teams, Outlook, Excel, Meeting. Microsoft-stack only, per Ben's call on 2026-04-22.
  • No blue anywhere. Blue is reserved for the Unifize platform layer that arrives in E2.
  • Hot accent (orange-red) used only for closure boxes, orphan counts, and "reassemble?" states. Earns its weight by being rare.
  • No em dashes in any label. Periods, middle dots, and commas only.
  • Five-second rule: each sketch should land "explosion plus failed reassembly" before any body copy is read.
  • Same sample event as the other explorations (CAPA-241) so the family reads as one system.

Suggested short list

  • 01 Fountain explosion: the most visceral. Physical feeling of a thing bursting apart. Best if we want the hero to "hurt."
  • 02 Branching tree: the most quantifiable. 1 to 5 to 15 to 30 is a chant a CFO can read. Use as the hero if the story is "scale of the problem."
  • 03 Thread delta: the most elegant metaphor. Risk: most chart-like. Use as a mid-scroll section, not a hero.
  • 04 Closure magnet: the most CFO-friendly. Orphan count is the proof artifact. Pairs well as a second section after 01 or 02 to quantify what they just felt.

Open questions before we pick one

  • Does the brief's "governed thread as alternative path" belong here (violating Ben's E1 constraint) or does it stay in E2 as originally locked? Default here is E2.
  • For the numbers (5 above, 30 leaves, 24 threads, 42 fragments, 14 orphans): are any of these defensible? If one of these reaches the homepage, it needs a citation, not a placeholder.
  • Do we lean into the closure-failure frame (Sketch 04) or the explosion frame (Sketch 01) as the E1 hero? They answer different questions: "what happened?" vs. "what does it cost you?"
